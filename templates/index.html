How accurate is it, really?

The live monitor can't tell you whether its number is correct — only whether it looks steady. Here you can find out, by replaying a recorded video and comparing what we measure against a pulse reading you already trust.

How Far Off Each Method Was

MAE is the headline: typical error in BPM. RMSE punishes occasional big misses, so RMSE far above MAE means rare bad failures rather than steady drift. MAPE expresses error as a percentage so it compares across people. r asks whether the method tracks changes even if it reads consistently high or low, and Bias shows that direction. 95% limits is the range almost every individual reading falls inside — the figure a clinical comparison turns on.

Why It Matters

Every heartbeat pushes blood into your face and changes its colour by an amount far too small to see. A camera can measure it. Keeping an eye on your resting heart rate over time is one of the simplest ways to notice stress, poor sleep or illness early.

Tips for Accurate Results

  • 💡Use even lighting. Natural, diffuse light works best — avoid heavy backlighting.
  • 👤Stay centered. Keep your face fully inside the frame.
  • 🤫Keep still. Avoid sudden movement and talking during the 13 s calibration and the reading.
  • 👓Remove glasses. Glare and reflections corrupt the skin mask.
  • 🏠Controlled environment. Indoors, away from flickering light sources.

Academic Disclaimer

This tool is for information and curiosity only. It is not a medical device and must not be used to diagnose or monitor any health condition. If something about your heart rate worries you, please talk to a doctor.
